title: "How to blog post?"
---

How do you write, and more importantly, how do you write blog posts? For Starters one may want to try to keep themself to the subject matter I think, in this post I deliberately picked 'How to blog post?' because I wanted to write about it. I haven't made that many posts on here yet so it kinda make sense that the first few posts are a bit about learning and setting out a foundation for later posts. 

To learn blogging properly I believe AI can act as a good springboard, it can give tips about how to structure or give links to tutorials related to whatever you are going to write about. As long as you don't plagiarize the AI it can be a great tool for learning most things, even writing blog posts. I do not mean that one should avoid auto-generating text, but it should be clear in any text who the author is. This can also be a gray area as sometimes you need to read up on a topic to understand it, whilst doing so, it can be easy to accidentally memorize a text without fully understanding it and thus end up partially copy-pasting what someone else has written. The best is if one is able to use their own words to explain something, or even better, artistically express it creatively in new ways that hasn't really been done before.

One way to use AI as a springboard is to do so in iterations. The first iteration, let's call it iteration zero, one just asks the AI how a skeleton of a post is structured in very general terms. Now there probably is no one-and-only kind of skeleton for blog posts so the AI comes back with some new set of questions instead direct answers. Then the next iteration is answering those questions yourself which leads to some kind of new prompt where the AI eventually asks you to select a topic. This is basically where the real work begins because now you have to chose something to actually write about. What I'm getting at here is that in each step of the writing process the AI can iteratively be used to guide you in the direction the work should proceed in. Once the work is finished there is also another iterative process of refining the text, especially important seems to be to "remove unecessary text". At least that is what I've famous writers claim to be important. If do not remove unecessary text then you most likely have some text that makes the text more voluminous and if it is too big with too little actual content then humans will not be willing to read it or it is going to rather difficult doing so. On the other hand AI can read way more than humans so maybe it is not as important to remove or refine text as it once used to. If a human doesn't find your text very easy to read then that person can just ask an AI to re-write it in whatever way they find good. That said, there are limits to what an AI can produce. One would think that an AI can write anything, just like a human can write text in any fashion, but in reality there is a strict boundary for what AIs can or can not do even if that boundary is not entirely obvious. LLMs use deep machine learning and the thing about that way to learn is that you optimize for one single outcome, not a nuance of different outcomes. Meanwhile there are more humans than there are LLMs and each human can have their own optimized way of writing or multiple different ways. Anyways I will try to wrap this text up. In short, AIs are a great tool for helping writing text but it shouldn't write text for us. If we haven't written something ourself then we may not even understand what is being written. Hopefully, this will help motivate both me and the reader to write more and not let AI do everything.
